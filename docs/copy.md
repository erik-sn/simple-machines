# Copy: the placard text

The book's captions live in `frontend/src/book/chapters.ts`. They are written from the dossier in `docs/research/history/`; this file is the style guide for new captions and the record of which dossier claims each chapter used and which it deliberately left out, so the book can be extended without re-reading the dossier.

## Style

- Placard prose: present tense, plain words, confident, no hedging, no exclamation marks, no lists inside a caption, no em dashes (a plain dash if unavoidable, otherwise restructure). Never mention AI or agents.
- Length: a caption is one or two paragraphs (each string in `text` renders as one paragraph) and must fit three lines at a 34em measure. Keep a stage's text under 230 characters in total; prefer under 180. Measure before committing.
- Titles: a stage may carry a short `title` (two to five words) for the beats; chapter openings and the Theatre's first stage carry none. Titles are sentence case. Numbers in captions are figures ("in 1586", "14 spheres", "5 powers"); a title may spell a number as a word ("Three hundred men") because figures sit badly in the italic display line.
- Quotations are short, verbatim from the dossier's [P] or [S] wording, and attributed inside the sentence ("Galileo writes", "Heron calls it"); never footnote numbers, never quotation marks around a paraphrase.
- Dates appear when they carry the story ("in 1577", "about AD 320"); otherwise leave them out.
- Reliability: facts and dates come only from claims the dossier tags [P] or [S]. A [W] claim may frame an uncontroversial point (a zip's slider is a wedge; Besson and Ramelli published theatres of machines) but never supplies a date, a number, or an attribution. [U] is never used. [derived] numbers are fine when the dossier shows the arithmetic.
- Real names for authors and plates where the dossier supports them: Heron, Pappus, Vitruvius, Pliny, Jordanus, Guidobaldo, Stevin, Galileo, Agricola, Besson, Ramelli, Zonca, Leonardo. Modern scholars (Coulton, Drachmann, Dijksterhuis) are not named on a placard; their findings are stated without attribution.
- Modes: `still` for the opening of a chapter and for the closing beat (the drawing rests until touched); `driven` for history beats and the physics stage (the reader watches the demonstration); `free` for "Try it", the introduction's "How to read", and the Theatre. A "Try it" caption names what to grab, what to tune in the settings, and what to watch in the margin (Load, Ideal advantage, Effort, Advantage).
- Variants name an arrangement the scene supports: lever `class1` (default), `class2`, `class3`; wheel-and-axle default and `windlass`; pulley `fixed` (default), `movable`, `tackle`; inclined-plane default and `cart`; wedge default and `log`; screw `jack` (default) and `press`. An unknown variant falls back to the default.
- Stage ids are short kebab-case and unique within a chapter. Chapter slugs, kinds, numerals, and titles are fixed. Each chapter has five to seven stages: an opening, history beats, the physics in words, "Try it", a closing beat.
- Coupling: `e2e/tests/reading.spec.ts` walks the introduction by stage title ("The trade" on page 2, "Archimedes at Syracuse" on page 3, "How to read" on page 6) and expects six introduction stages before `/lever`. Changing the introduction changes that journey.
- Settings named in "Try it" captions: the lever's (fulcrum, pin friction) match its `SettingSpec` labels. The wheel-and-axle, pulley, inclined-plane, wedge, and screw captions name settings (wheel and axle size, sheaves, angle and friction, wedge angle, handle length, thread pitch, friction) that the physics modules still being built must expose under those meanings, or the captions must be updated when they land.
