# Reference images for Simple Machines

This folder holds downscaled reference images for the site's look: black ink drawings on old paper, period typography and paper texture, plus a second set of ruled engineering drawings and early grids for the site's second theme. Every image is public domain or under an open license (CC0 or CC BY) as recorded by its source, so each one can also be shown on the site with the attribution given here. Sources with non-commercial or unclear terms (Gallica scans, museum sites that claim rights on reproductions, the ProQuest-scanned Koninklijke Bibliotheek copy of Stevin's 1586 Weeghconst) were left out on purpose.

How the files were made: originals were downloaded from the source given for each entry, then reduced so the longest side is at most 1800 px and saved as JPEG (quality 85, or lower where needed to stay under about 300 KB). The two files whose names begin with detail- are crops at the scan's native resolution. Wikimedia Commons throttles downloads of original files, so most Deutsche Fotothek images whose originals are 800 px wide were fetched as the 500 px standard thumbnail instead; their entries note the original size, and the direct file link still points to the original. Full-resolution originals are not kept in the repository; the direct file link in each entry points to them. Internet Archive links use the scan page index (n plus a number) of the copy cited, which is not the printed page number; printed page or plate numbers are given in the Source line when the page carries one, and are called unknown otherwise.

Slugs: people (portraits for the introduction and chapter openers), lever, wheel-and-axle, pulley, inclined-plane, wedge, screw, compound (theatres of machines and machines that combine several simple ones), paper-and-type (paper texture, title pages, printers' devices, line samples, grids and engineering drawings).

Per-source license basis, in short:

- Internet Archive scans by the Getty Research Institute and the Smithsonian Libraries: the books are sixteenth to eighteenth century printed works, public domain everywhere; the Getty and Smithsonian items are marked public domain or NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT in their Internet Archive metadata.
- Wellcome Collection: images tagged Public Domain Mark or CC BY 4.0 in the Wellcome catalogue API; the IIIF link is the direct file.
- Wikimedia Commons: the license field of each file's imageinfo record was checked (Public domain, CC0 or CC BY); the Commons page is the source page. Files donated by the Deutsche Fotothek (SLUB Dresden) carry the Fotothek PD-old-100 license template and their provenance (book, page or figure, technique, shelfmark) comes from the Fotothek record embedded on the Commons page.
- Rijksmuseum, Metropolitan Museum of Art and British Library files on Commons: CC0.
- Library of Congress, National Archives (via DPLA), National Library of Medicine, Houghton Library, National Library of Poland (Polona), Tresoar: public domain reproductions of public domain works, as stated on the Commons page.

Targets that were looked for and not taken: a period figure of Heron's Mechanica (only later editions of the Pneumatica and Automata were found under an open license); Galileo's Le Mecaniche figures (the only Commons copies are small redrawn figures of uncertain provenance and the Fisher Library scan carries no rights statement); Stevin's 1586 De Beghinselen der Weeghconst title page with the clootcrans (every open copy found is a ProQuest Early European Books scan, so the 1634 Leiden Oeuvres, scanned by the Smithsonian, stands in); Leupold's Theatrum Machinarum Generale (the Commons set is photographs of text pages from a 1982 facsimile); the British Museum's Besson impressions (CC BY-NC-SA); Guidobaldo's 1577 Latin Mechanicorum liber (only Google Books derived scans found, so the 1581 Italian Le mechaniche from the Getty is used); a Guidobaldo portrait under an open license (the Fondazione Zeri photograph on Commons is CC BY-SA); pre-1800 printed graph paper (Playfair's 1786 engraved grid stands in).
