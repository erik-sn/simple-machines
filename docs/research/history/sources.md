# Sources: annotated bibliography

Reliability notes use the tags of the other files: [P] primary source, [S] scholarly secondary, [W] reference-work level, [U] not accessed in this dossier and cited from the literature. "Seen" means the text was read for this dossier in the edition or page named; "not seen" means it is cited because the field relies on it and should be consulted before the site quotes it.

## Primary sources, ancient

- Pseudo-Aristotle, Mechanical Problems (Mechanica), c. 300 BC. English: W. S. Hett, Aristotle, Minor Works (Loeb 1936), public domain at penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Aristotle/Mechanica*.html; E. S. Forster, Works of Aristotle vol. 6 (Oxford 1913), archive.org/details/worksofaristotle06arisuoft. Seen. [P] The source for the lever (3), oar (4), windlass (13), wedge (17, 19), pulley (18) and the wheel paradox (24). Reliable text; authorship disputed (Peripatetic, possibly Straton; Winter argues Archytas).
- Archimedes, On the Equilibrium of Planes I. Heath, The Works of Archimedes (Cambridge 1897); extract at mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Archimedes_on_statics/. Seen in extract. [P] Propositions 6-7 are the law of the lever. Heiberg's Teubner text (1910-15) is the critical edition.
- Herodotus II.125, tr. Macaulay, lexundria.com/hdt/2.125/gd. Seen. [P] The pyramid "machines made of short pieces of timber."
- Diodorus Siculus I.34 and V.37, tr. Oldfather (Loeb), penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diodorus_Siculus/. Seen. [P] The water screw attributed to Archimedes; written c. 60-30 BC, two centuries after the fact.
- Strabo, Geography XVII.1.30 and 52, penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/17A3*.html. Seen. [P] Screws in Egypt, no inventor named.
- Vitruvius, De architectura X, c. 30-20 BC. English: M. H. Morgan (Harvard 1914), at perseus.tufts.edu (text 1999.02.0073) and lexundria.com/vitr/10.x/mg; Latin at penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/10*.html. Seen. [P] The only ancient handbook: definition of machine (X.1), cranes (X.2), lever and steelyard (X.3), watermill (X.5), water screw (X.6), catapult wedges (X.12); VII praef. 14 lists the writers on machines. Morgan's translation is reliable; his "banauson" for the third class is a manuscript reading editors emend.
- Heron of Alexandria, Mechanics, c. AD 60-70, Arabic of Qusta ibn Luqa (862-866 per one Iranian study; 9th century for certain). Editions: Carra de Vaux, Les mécaniques ou l'élévateur de Héron d'Alexandrie (Journal Asiatique 1893; Paris 1894; reprint Belles Lettres 1988 with Hill and Drachmann), partial view at sourcelibrary.org; L. Nix and W. Schmidt, Heronis Alexandrini opera II.1 (Teubner 1900, Arabic with German), archive.org/details/heronisalexandri21hero. Seen in Nix's German and in the Cohen-Drabkin and Pearse extracts. [P] There is no complete English translation; every English quotation in this dossier is a working translation from Nix or from Cohen and Drabkin (who translated Nix). Reliability of the text: good for content, uncertain for exact wording, since it passed Greek to Arabic to German to English.
- Pappus of Alexandria, Collection VIII, c. AD 320. F. Hultsch, Pappi Alexandrini Collectionis quae supersunt (Berlin 1876-78), Greek with Latin, archive.org/details/pappialexandrin04pappgoog and pappialexandrin05pappgoog; Ver Eecke's French (1933); Commandino's Latin (Pesaro 1588). Seen in Hultsch's Latin column. [P] Preface, five powers (p. 1116), Archimedes' saying with the baroulkos (prop. 10, pp. 1060-61), inclined plane (prop. 9). Hultsch flags section XXXI (the five powers) as possibly interpolated from Heron.
- Plutarch, Marcellus 14-19, c. AD 100. Perrin (Loeb 1917) at penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Marcellus*.html and lexundria.com/plut_marc/14/prr; Greek at el.wikisource.org; Dryden at classics.mit.edu. Seen. [P] The boast, the ship, the claw, the death. Three centuries after the events, rhetorical, but the standard narrative source.
- Polybius VIII.3-7, 2nd century BC, tr. Paton (Loeb), penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/8*.html. Seen. [P] The earliest and most sober account of the claw and the siege.
- Livy XXIV.34 and XXV.31, Augustan. Latin at thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.24.shtml; English at perseus.tufts.edu. Seen. [P] The claw with its lead counterweight; the death.
- Athenaeus, Deipnosophistae V.206d-209e, c. AD 200, quoting Moschion (lost, c. 250-220 BC?). Yonge (1854) at attalus.org/old/athenaeus5b.html; Gulick (Loeb) at penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Athenaeus/5C*.html. Seen. [P] The Syracusia, the helix launch, the screw bilge pump. A late compiler quoting a lost source; some scholars doubt the ship's historicity.
- Valerius Maximus VIII.7.ext.7, c. AD 30, thelatinlibrary.com/valmax8.html. Seen. [P] "Noli, obsecro, istum disturbare," the oldest form of the "circles" saying.
- Pliny, Natural History XVIII.317, XXXIII.71-72, XXXVI.14. Latin at penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Pliny_the_Elder/. Seen for 33 and 36; 18.317 via Gichon's article. [P] Screw presses "within the last hundred years"; iron wedges in mines; the Silenus in the Parian block.
- Diogenes Laertius VIII.83, en.wikisource.org. Seen. [P] Archytas and mechanics; no screw.
- Sumerian Farmer's Instructions, ETCSL 5.6.3, etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/section5/tr563.htm. Seen. [P] "The wedge of your plough share."

Also Drachmann, "Heron's Screwcutter," JHS 56 (1936) 72-77 (Cambridge Core), and "Fragments from Archimedes in Heron's Mechanics," Centaurus 8 (1963) 91-146, both cited from the literature.

## How to use this bibliography

- Quote ancient authors only from the editions named; where this dossier gives a working translation from German, Latin, Italian or Dutch, say so on the site or find the standard English (Drake for Galileo, Dijksterhuis for Stevin, Drachmann or Cohen-Drabkin for Heron).
- Check every Leonardo folio number and every Ramelli plate number not listed above against the facsimiles before publication.
- Where this dossier says "sources disagree," the placard should hedge; the README lists the disagreements in one place.
